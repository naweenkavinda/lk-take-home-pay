      The law behind each number

      Each formula the calculator uses, with the provision it comes from. Numbers in brackets point to the source list.

      APIT on monthly salary (IRD Table 01)

      Residents and non-resident citizens get personal relief of Rs 1,800,000 a year (Rs 150,000 a month). Taxable income is then charged at 6%, 18%, 24%, 30% and 36% [1]. IRD folds the relief into one formula on gross monthly pay M [3]:

      M ≤ 150,000 → 0 150,000 – 233,333 → 6% × M − 9,000 233,333 – 275,000 → 18% × M − 37,000 275,000 – 316,667 → 24% × M − 53,500 316,667 – 358,333 → 30% × M − 72,500 above 358,333 → 36% × M − 94,000

      M includes basic pay, all allowances, overtime and the value of benefits in kind. Employee EPF is not deducted first: "No deduction shall be made" from employment income (IRA s.10(1)(a)) [2].

      Bonus and other lump sums (Table 02)

      A bonus is taxed on the year as a whole. D is the year's regular pay plus all lump sums. IRD's annual formula is applied to D, and the tax already due on regular pay is subtracted [4]:

      D = 12 × M + bonus Tax on bonus = (r × D − K) − 12 × Table01(M) r/K: 6%/108,000 · 18%/444,000 · 24%/642,000 30%/870,000 · 36%/1,128,000 (D ≤ 1.8M → 0)

      IRD's own example gives Rs 36,000: M = 170,000 with a bonus of 600,000. This calculator reproduces it. It assumes the same salary all year and no earlier lump sums.

      Foreign nationals and second jobs (Tables 04 & 07)

      Non-resident non-citizens get no relief and APIT is their final tax [5]:

      ≤ 83,333 → 6%×M · ≤ 125,000 → 18%×M − 10,000 ≤ 166,667 → 24%×M − 17,500 · ≤ 208,333 → 30%×M − 27,500 above → 36%×M − 40,000

      Secondary employment is taxed at one flat rate on the whole second salary. The rate depends on primary-job pay and on combined pay: 0%, 6%, 18%, 24%, 30% or 36%. Without a declaration the rate is 36%, and non-residents pay 36% [6].

      EPF and ETF

      EPF earnings = basic + COLA + similar allowances Employee EPF = 8% × EPF earnings (deducted) Employer EPF = 12% × EPF earnings (on top) Employer ETF = 3% × EPF earnings (on top)

      The rates come from EPF Act s.10(1)–(2) [7] and ETF Act s.16(1) [8]. "Earnings" (EPF s.47, ETF s.44) covers wages, cost-of-living and similar allowances, holiday pay, meal allowances and commission. Overtime and bonuses are not in that list, so they are left out. There is no ceiling. The employer may not recover its share from pay (EPF s.20, ETF s.30). Its EPF/ETF contributions are not taxable to the employee (IRA s.5) [2].

      Overtime (Shop & Office Act)

      Hourly rate = (basic + COLA) ÷ 240 Overtime = hourly × 1.5 × hours Public-holiday work = 2× the daily rate

      Normal hours are 8 a day and 45 a week (Act s.3). The Shop & Office Regulations cap overtime at 12 hours a week, set the 1.5× minimum and the ÷240 hourly rate, and count only salary plus cost-of-living allowance (and any prescribed allowance) in the base (Regs. 6–9). Work on a public holiday is paid double (s.7(2)) and on a Poya day at 1.5× (s.7B) [9].

      Gratuity, minimum wage and benefits

      Gratuity = ½ × last monthly salary × completed years Monthly accrual shown = (basic + COLA + allowances) ÷ 24

      Gratuity is owed after 5 years' service by employers with 15 or more workmen. It must be paid within 30 days of leaving (Payment of Gratuity Act s.5–6, s.20) [10]. The national minimum wage is Rs 30,000 a month (Rs 1,200 a day) from 1 Jan 2026. The old Rs 3,500 budgetary relief allowance is now merged into wages (Acts No. 9 and No. 10 of 2025, with the new rate set by Act No. 11 of 2025) [11][12]. Benefit-in-kind values follow IRD SEC/2023/E/02 [3].
